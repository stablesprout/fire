Why free?

Financial literacy should not be a privilege. The gap between people who understand how money compounds, how fees erode returns, and how to build a plan — and people who do not — is one of the most consequential gaps that almost nobody talks about honestly.

The knowledge exists. It is taught in finance degrees and used daily by professionals. It just is not being shared clearly, freely, or without an agenda. StableSprout is an attempt to fix a small part of that.

What the tools are built on

Every calculator is grounded in established personal finance and investment concepts. None of it is new. It just has not been made this easy to interact with before.

Time value of money
A euro today is worth more than a euro tomorrow. This single idea sits underneath every financial decision you will ever make.
Compound interest
When your returns start earning their own returns, growth stops being linear. Time is the variable that changes everything.
Inflation
Cash that sits still loses value every year. At 3% inflation, €50.000 becomes €37.000 of real purchasing power in 10 years.
Risk and return
Higher returns always come with higher risk. There are no exceptions. Understanding the trade-off is where investing actually begins.
Diversification
Spreading across uncorrelated assets reduces risk without giving up proportional return. It is the only genuine edge available to everyone.
Asset allocation
How you split between stocks, bonds, and cash drives more of your long-run outcome than which specific funds you pick inside each bucket.
Loss aversion
Losses feel about twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good. This wiring causes most investors to sell low and buy high.
Recency bias
We overweight recent events and underweight long-run patterns. Every market panic looks obvious in hindsight. Every recovery does too.
Net worth
Everything you own minus everything you owe. The number that tells you where you actually stand — and the one most people have never calculated.
The 4% rule
Withdraw 4% of your portfolio per year and, historically, it lasts 30+ years in retirement. The foundation every FIRE calculation is built on.
Savings rate
The biggest lever you actually control. How much you save matters far more than how cleverly you invest it.
Rebalancing
Selling what has grown and buying what has lagged to maintain your target allocation. The discipline that forces you to buy low and sell high.

What changes the outcome most?
Three concepts that explain everything
📐
Rule of 72
Divide 72 by your return rate = years to double. At 7% that is ~10 years.
🏔️
The snowball
The longer it rolls, the bigger it gets. The last decade builds the fortune.
💸
The 4% rule
Withdraw 4%/yr of your portfolio. Historically lasts 30+ years in retirement.
The hidden cost of fees
John Bogle's core insight: You can't control market returns. You can control costs. A 1% annual fee sounds small — over 30 years it can consume a third of your final balance.

What to look for in a fund
✅
Index funds
Typically 0.03–0.2% annual fees. Own the whole market. No one picking stocks.
⚠️
Actively managed
Typically 0.5–2% fees. A fund manager picks stocks — and usually underperforms the index after costs.
❌
High-cost funds
Anything above 1% is eating a significant share of your returns. Check the expense ratio before you buy.

How to protect against inflation
📈
Invest in equities
Stocks have historically returned 7–10% annually — well above inflation. The risk is short-term volatility, not long-term loss of value.
🏠
Real assets
Property, REITs, and commodities tend to rise with inflation. They provide a hedge when cash is losing value.
💰
High-yield savings for emergency fund only
Keep 3–6 months of expenses in cash. Beyond that, cash is a liability that inflation erodes every year.

Why 3–6 months, not more, not less
⚠️
Under 3 months — too exposed
A single job loss or medical bill wipes you out and forces debt. This is the most common reason people raid their investments at the worst time.
✅
3–6 months — the sweet spot
Covers most emergencies. Freelancers and single-income households should aim for 6. Stable dual-income households can get away with 3.
💭
Over 12 months — opportunity cost
Cash beyond 6 months earns less than inflation. Once funded, additional savings should go to investments, not the emergency fund.
